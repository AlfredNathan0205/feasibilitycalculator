# Guide for Approvers

You are asked to approve or reject the sign-offs routed to your role, for example PPD resource,
marketing resource, a short deadline, a creative approach, or a Tier T brief. If you are the
General Manager, see "The General Manager's veto" at the end.

> You need the **Approver** access role plus the specific approval role (for example PPD
> Manager). Without both you see nothing to approve. Ask an Admin if something is missing.

## 1. Being asked

When a brief needs your sign-off you get an email titled **"Sign-off needed — <customer reference>"**. It lists
each sign-off that is waiting on you, the customer deadline, and a link to your **Approvals** page. Everyone who
holds the role is emailed, and you get one email per brief, however many of your roles it needs. Emails can arrive
up to about five minutes after the brief is submitted.

You are **not** emailed about your own briefs, and not when the brief was declined outright on score. You are not
reminded again: if you are waiting on something, open **Approvals** in the left menu. A sign-off you can't decide
because you submitted the brief goes to the Admins, who are emailed instead.

## 2. Your approvals page

**Approvals** lists everything waiting for you, grouped by sign-off type. Each item shows the customer,
tier and value, the score, and the commercial position ("commercially Auto-approved / Pending /
Declined").

For each item you can:

- **Approve**. Optionally add a comment.
- **Reject**. A **comment is mandatory** and should say why: it is shown to the submitter on the brief.

When several items of the same type are waiting, tick the ones you want and press **Approve selected**
to approve them in one go. (Bulk action approves only; reject items one at a time with a comment.)
Open the customer reference first if you want the full brief: inputs, score breakdown and the other sign-offs.

## 3. What your decision does

- **Approve:** the sign-off is cleared. When it was the last one outstanding, and the brief isn't
  commercially declined, the brief becomes **Approved** and the Approval Code is issued. The submitter (and the
  Account Manager of record) is emailed the code.
- **Reject:** the sign-off is marked rejected with your comment, and the submitter is emailed with your comment. A
  rejected sign-off does not clear the brief, so it stays without an Approval Code. (Talk to the submitter; if the brief should be stopped
  outright, ask the General Manager or an Admin.)

Decisions can't be undone from the Approvals page. If you made a mistake, tell an Admin.

## 4. If you are named as a pre-approving manager

A submitter can declare that you already agreed a sign-off. You get an email titled
_Pre-approval declared on your behalf_, with the submitter's comment and a **revoke link**.

- If it's correct, **do nothing**. After the window (72 hours) it stands.
- If it's **not** correct, open the link and press **Yes, revoke this pre-approval**. The link works **once**.
  The sign-off returns to the queue and any Approval Code already issued for that brief is withdrawn.
  The submitter is told.

## 5. Things you cannot do, by design

- Approve a sign-off that isn't routed to your role.
- Approve a brief **you submitted** or one submitted for you as Account Manager. If every holder of a role is in that
  position, the sign-off is **escalated to an Admin** and recorded as such.

## 6. The General Manager's veto

If you hold the **General Manager (veto)** role (Pauline Holmes):

- You can see **every brief** (not the audit trail), filter the **Briefs** list by Account Manager, and open the
  **Dashboard** (without the CSV/XLSX exports).
- Nothing is ever routed to you, so nothing waits on you.
- On the **Approvals** page, under **Veto**, you see every sign-off that is still open on any brief, whoever
  it is routed to. Type a **reason** (required) and press **Veto**.

A veto:

1. marks that sign-off rejected with your reason,
2. **declines the brief** (any Approval Code is withdrawn, and no one can approve the brief's remaining
   sign-offs),
3. emails the submitter, and
4. is recorded in the audit trail with your name.

Only an **Admin** can reverse a veto, and they must give a reason. Reversal returns the sign-off to
the approver's queue and the brief to Pending. Use the veto for genuine reasons (capacity, a conflict with
strategy), not as a routine step: it is visible to everyone who can see the brief.
