# Guide for Account Managers and Sales Coordinators

You submit briefs and follow them through to an Approval Code.

> Who can submit: people with the **Account Manager** or **Sales Coordinator** role. If you
> don't see a **New brief** button on your home page, ask an Admin to add the role.

## 1. Your home page

After signing in, **Your briefs** shows the 10 briefs you most recently submitted with their score,
status and Approval Code (once issued). Click a customer reference to open the brief.
The left-hand menu has **Briefs** (search everything you can see) and, for people who also
approve, **Approvals**.

## 2. Submitting a brief

Click **New brief**. The form has three steps.

### Step 1 — Brief details

| Field                                   | What to enter                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Customer reference**                  | The customer's name or your reference for the opportunity. You will search by this later.                   |
| **Customer tier**                       | A, B, C, D, or T (Target account), as defined by CPL's rules. The list comes from the live rule set.        |
| **Value potential (£)**                 | Expected annual value in pounds. Zero makes every value-based score component zero.                         |
| **New / Rework**                        | New; Rework (Of Selling); or Rework (Non-Selling).                                                          |
| **Brief type**                          | Exclusive, Competitive or ProActive.                                                                        |
| **Customer approval**                   | Direct, or Deferred/Unknown.                                                                                |
| **Creative approach**                   | Library Only, Starting Point, or Creation/Unknown.                                                          |
| **Deadline**                            | The customer's deadline. It must be in the future.                                                          |
| **PV reference**                        | Optional project-vault reference.                                                                           |
| **Niche / Fine Fragrance pre-approved** | Tick only if it genuinely is. A **rationale is required**. This makes the brief commercially Auto-approved. |
| **Strategic priority**                  | Tick if it is a strategic priority. A **rationale is required**. It adds to the score.                      |
| **Resources**                           | Tick **Marketing**, **PPD** and/or **GCMS** if the project needs them. Each one always needs a sign-off.    |

Click **Next: Approvals**.

### Step 2 — Approvals (the live preview)

You now see, before you commit to anything:

- the **score** and the **commercial decision** (Auto-approved, Pending or Declined),
- a bar for each part of the score, so you can see what is driving it,
- **Will require approval from**: every sign-off this brief raises and who gives it.

If something surprises you, press **Back** and check your answers. This is the best time to
spot a wrong tier or a mistyped value.

**Pre-approval (optional).** If a manager has _already_ agreed a sign-off with you (for
example, Jo Lambert has told you the short deadline is fine), you can declare it:

1. Tick the pre-approval box under that sign-off.
2. Choose the **manager** from the list of people who hold that role.
3. Write a **comment** saying what was agreed.

The brief proceeds as if that sign-off were cleared, and the manager you named receives an email with a one-time **revoke link**. If
they do nothing, the pre-approval stands. They have **72 hours** to revoke; revoking returns the
sign-off to the queue and **withdraws any Approval Code** already issued. Use pre-approval only
when the conversation really happened: it is recorded under your name, and CPL reports on how
often it is used and revoked.

### Step 3 — Review & submit

Check the summary, then **Submit brief**. You are taken to the result.

## 3. What happens next

The brief's status is one of:

| Status       | Meaning                                                                     | What you do                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Approved** | Commercially OK and every sign-off cleared. The **Approval Code** is shown. | Quote the code; you can go ahead.                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Pending**  | Waiting on the commercial decision and/or one or more sign-offs.            | Wait. **Approvers are not emailed automatically** when a sign-off is raised, so tell them (the brief page shows which role each sign-off is waiting on) and check back on the brief. |
| **Declined** | Commercially declined, or vetoed by the General Manager.                    | Do not proceed. See the brief for the reason. A new brief can be raised if the facts change.                                                                                         |

Important: a brief can show **commercially Auto-approved** while still **Pending** because a
sign-off is outstanding. There is no Approval Code until all sign-offs clear.

There is **no email when your brief is approved**. Open the brief (or **Your briefs**) to see the status and
the Approval Code. You **are** emailed when a pre-approval you declared is revoked, when a brief of yours is
vetoed by the General Manager, and when such a veto is reversed. Emails come from the Feasibility Calculator
mailbox and can arrive up to about five minutes after the event.

## 4. Finding and checking a brief

- **Briefs** (left menu) lets you search by customer or PV reference, and filter by status and tier.
- Click a brief to see its inputs, score breakdown, commercial decision, the sign-offs and who decided
  each (with their comments), and the Approval Code.
- You see **your own briefs** (and, if you are also an approver, briefs with a sign-off for your role).
  You do not see the audit trail; Admins and Auditors do.

## 5. Common questions

**Can I edit a brief after submitting?** No. A submitted brief is a record. If a fact was wrong,
submit a new brief (and tell the approvers so they ignore the earlier one).

**Why was my brief declined?** A score at or below the decline threshold (currently 30) is declined.
The breakdown on the brief shows what pulled it down. Competitive briefs and non-selling rework
contribute nothing to value-based parts of the score; ProActive subtracts.

**Why does an A-tier brief not need approval but a T-tier one does?** Tier T is a Target account:
by CPL's rule it always goes to the Commercial Director (once Tier T has been switched on).

**The approver is on leave.** Tell an Admin; they can add a second holder to the role.

**I submitted something I shouldn't have.** Contact an Admin straight away; nothing can be deleted, but
the people who need to know can be told and a veto can stop it.
